IWR6843AOP mmWave Radar - Distance vs. Target Material
Test Conditions, Results and Findings
========================================================================
Test date: 2026-10-13, approx. 12:02 - 12:23 local time (single session)
Raw data:  IWR_Radar_Distance\logs\experiment\   (summary.csv + per-trial files)
Figures:   IWR_Radar_Distance\report\figures\     (fig1 - fig7, PNG, 200 dpi)


1. TEST CONDITIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hardware
  - Radar:        TI IWR6843AOPEVM (60 GHz, antenna-on-package, 3 TX x 4 RX)
  - Connection:   USB via Silicon Labs CP2105 dual UART
                  CLI port  = COM8 ("Enhanced"), 115200 baud
                  Data port = COM9 ("Standard"), 921600 baud
  - Host:         Windows 10 PC, Python 3.13 + pyserial (custom scripts)

Firmware / configuration
  - Stock TI mmWave SDK 3.5 Out-of-Box demo (xWR68xx_AOP)
  - Config exported from TI mmWave Demo Visualizer 3.5.0,
    scene "best range resolution" (default_config.cfg)
  - Start frequency 60 GHz, slope 70 MHz/us, 256 ADC samples at 5209 ksps
      -> range bin width 4.36 cm, max unambiguous range 9.02 m
  - TDM-MIMO, all 3 TX / 4 RX enabled (channelCfg 15 7 0), 16 chirp loops
  - Frame period 100 ms (10 frames/s)
  - Detection: CFAR-CASO in range, 15 dB threshold (range and Doppler),
    peak grouping on, static clutter removal OFF
  - Range-bias compensation NOT calibrated (compRangeBiasAndRxChanPhase
    range bias = 0.0)
  - ONE CHANGE from the Visualizer export: DC range-signature calibration
    enabled (calibDcRangeSig -1 1 -5 8 256) to suppress antenna coupling at
    close range. It averages the first 256 frames after start and subtracts
    that static signal from range bins -5..+8 (out to ~0.35 m).

Geometry
  - Radar mounted facing straight down at the desk.
  - Radar-to-desk distance: 0.51 m (tape measure, radar board face to desk
    top).
  - Each box placed centred directly beneath the radar.
  - Box dimensions (as given). ASSUMPTION: the first dimension listed is the
    vertical height; please confirm boxes were oriented this way.

      Box         Height   Top surface (L x W)   Top area    Geometric distance
                                                             radar -> box top
      Metal       135 mm   95 x 95 mm            9,025 mm2   0.375 m
      Plastic      45 mm   225 x 155 mm         34,875 mm2   0.465 m
      Felt         80 mm   100 x 80 mm           8,000 mm2   0.430 m
      Cardboard    35 mm   65 x 110 mm           7,150 mm2   0.475 m

  - Stacked conditions (second box laid flat, centred on top of the metal box):
      Cardboard on metal  170 mm total  -> 0.340 m
      Plastic on metal    180 mm total  -> 0.330 m
      Felt on metal       215 mm total  -> 0.295 m

Procedure
  1. Sensor configured once for the whole session; 30 s settle/calibration
     with the area under the radar empty (301 frames received).
  2. Baseline: empty desk.
  3. Single boxes: 3 rounds of metal -> plastic -> felt -> cardboard. The box
     was removed and re-placed between every trial (repeatability).
  4. Stacked boxes: 2 trials each of cardboard, plastic, then felt on top of
     the metal box. Top box re-placed between trials for plastic and felt;
     cardboard-on-metal trial 2 was run WITHOUT re-placing.
  5. Final empty-desk baseline (drift check).
  - Each trial = 15 s capture (~150 frames). 20 trials, 3,011 frames total.
  - Operator stepped away from the beam before each capture.

Data processing definitions
  - "Detection": the CFAR detector reported a point within +/-0.10 m of the
    geometric distance to the box top. If more than one, the highest-SNR
    point was used.
  - "Measured height" = (radar's mean desk reading) - (radar's mean box
    reading). Using the radar's own desk reading as the reference cancels
    the fixed range offset (Finding 1).
  - "Peak signal" = the highest value of the frame-averaged range profile
    within +/-3 bins (+/-13 cm) of the target's expected range. Range profile
    values converted from the SDK's Q9 log2 format to dB
    (x 20*log10(2)/512). These are RELATIVE dB, not calibrated radar cross
    section.
  - Per-point SNR values reported by the radar were recorded but are not used
    to compare materials (see Finding 12).

3. FINDINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
F1. Fixed range offset of about +6 cm
    The radar read the desk at 0.567 m against a tape-measured 0.51 m
    (+5.7 cm). Every detected target showed a similar offset relative to its
    geometric distance: metal +6.1, plastic +5.8, cardboard +4.8, plastic on
    metal +6.3 cm. The spread between these (1.5 cm) is within one range bin.
    Likely causes: range-bias compensation not calibrated in this config,
    and the antenna's electrical reference point not being exactly at the
    board face. It is a constant offset, so it can be removed by subtracting
    it, by measuring heights relative to a reference surface (as done here),
    or with TI's measureRangeBiasAndRxChanPhase calibration procedure.

F2. Relative accuracy within one range bin (Fig. 5)
    Measured relative to the desk, the box heights came out within 1-9 mm of
    actual: metal 131 vs 135, plastic 44 vs 45, cardboard 44 vs 35, plastic
    on metal 174 vs 180 mm. All are within +/- half a range bin
    (+/-21.8 mm), which is the best this configuration can resolve.

F3. Readings are quantised to 4.36 cm steps; repeatability is exact at
    that resolution
    Every detection snapped to a range-bin centre, and each target landed in
    the same bin in every frame of every trial, including after the box was
    removed and re-placed. Precision is limited by bin size, not by noise.
    The start and end empty-desk baselines were identical (0.567 m; 45.34
    vs 45.46 dB), so there was no measurable drift over the ~20 min session.

F4. Range resolution limits what can be separated (Figs. 2, 5)
    The cardboard (35 mm) and plastic (45 mm) boxes both read 0.523 m. They
    are 10 mm apart in height, well under one bin, so the radar cannot tell
    them apart by distance. Similarly, with cardboard on the metal box the
    two top surfaces (35 mm apart) merged into a single broad hump in the
    range profile instead of a sharp peak. The detector then reported
    nothing (see F7).

F5. Reflection strength by material (Figs. 1, 3)
    Peak signal ranked metal (48.7 dB) > plastic (47.8) > cardboard (45.2)
    ~ bare desk (45.4) > felt (43.3). Metal, plastic and cardboard each
    produced a distinct peak at their top surface.
    IMPORTANT CAVEAT: signal strength also depends on range and on the size
    of the top surface, and these differed between boxes. Two comparisons
    still hold up despite that:
      - Metal was strongest even though its top area is about 1/4 of the
        plastic box's.
      - Felt was weaker than cardboard even though its top is larger and
        closer to the radar.
    Metal vs. plastic on material alone is not conclusive from this data.

F6. Felt was never detected and blocks what is behind it (Figs. 1-4, 6)
    Felt: 0 of 451 frames over 3 placements. Its range profile is flat and
    smeared (about 42-44 dB from 0.44 to 0.65 m) with no peak, so the CFAR
    detector, which needs a bin about 15 dB above its neighbours, never
    fired. The felt also suppressed the desk return under it (desk detected
    in 0 % of felt frames).
    Felt on metal: the metal-top return dropped from 48.7 dB (metal alone)
    to 40.0 and 38.2 dB, a loss of about 9-11 dB (mean ~9.7 dB) from the
    felt layer. Neither box top was detected. The felt is effectively
    invisible to this radar AND hides a strong reflector beneath it.
    Probable mechanism: porous, fibrous material that scatters diffusely and
    absorbs rather than reflecting from a smooth surface. (Construction of
    the felt box, solid vs. felt-covered, should be recorded in the report.)

F7. Cardboard is weakly reflective and largely transparent
    Alone, cardboard was detected reliably (100 %). On top of the metal box,
    the metal-top bin was still at 46.3 dB, at most ~2.5 dB below metal
    alone, versus ~9.7 dB for felt. The metal box's multipath ghost (F9) was
    detected in 79 % of frames THROUGH the cardboard. So cardboard passes
    most of the signal; its weak top return merged with the metal's (F4) and
    nothing was detected at the stack top.

F8. Plastic on metal was resolved
    The plastic top was detected one bin above the metal (0.393 m, 89 % of
    frames), giving a height of 174 vs 180 mm. Plastic's strong, sharp
    return is what let the detector separate it. The 11 % of missed frames
    are attributed to the plastic top being only ~1 bin from the metal top.

F9. Multipath "ghost ladder" from strong reflectors (Fig. 7)
    Metal and plastic produced repeated false targets farther away than the
    real box. The ghosts sit at k x (true distance) + offset, k = 2, 3, 4...,
    which is consistent with the signal bouncing back and forth between the
    box top and the flat radar board.
      - Metal: detections at 0.436, 0.785, 1.177, 1.526, 2.006, 2.268 m.
        Fitted spacing 0.388 m vs true distance 0.375 m.
      - Plastic: 0.523, 1.003 (in 86 % of frames), 1.483, 2.442 m.
        Fitted spacing 0.480 m vs true distance 0.465 m.
      - Cardboard, felt and the bare desk: no ghosts.
    The spacings match the true distances to within ~3.5 %, within the
    bin-quantisation uncertainty. Ghosts accounted for almost all of the
    1,054 detections recorded beyond 1.0 m.
    Practical impact: ghosts are always farther than the real target, so a
    "nearest object" reading is not fooled by them, but anything that uses
    all detections (multi-target tracking, point clouds) would see false
    objects. Possible mitigations (untested): tilt the radar slightly, add
    RF absorber around the antenna, or reject detections at integer
    multiples of a strong near target.

F10. Unexplained returns 4-9 cm beyond the desk surface (Fig. 6)
    Detections at 0.611 and 0.654 m appeared in 36-94 % of frames whenever a
    single box sat on the desk, but never with the empty desk. Source not
    identified. Leading hypothesis: structure beneath or inside the desk top
    that is normally masked by the strong desk-surface return, and becomes a
    local peak when a box shadows part of the desk. Not tested further.

F11. Stacks raise the near-range signal
    In all three stacked conditions, range bins 0.17-0.30 m rose by 5-10 dB
    compared with the metal box alone, and the rise was nearly identical
    regardless of the top material (Fig. 2). This points to geometry (a
    taller object closer to the radar) rather than material. It also raises
    the CFAR detector's local noise estimate near the target, making
    detection harder.

F12. The radar's per-point SNR is not a material measure
    The radar reported lower SNR for metal-box points (12-17 dB) than for
    the bare desk (35 dB) even though the metal's raw return was stronger.
    The SNR is computed against a LOCAL noise estimate, which other nearby
    returns inflate. The range-profile peak was used as the
    signal-strength measure instead.

F13. Undetected targets are in the data but below the detector threshold
    For felt and cardboard-on-metal, energy is visible in the range profile;
    it is the fixed 15 dB CFAR threshold and peak-shape requirement that
    rejects them. A lower threshold or a different detector might detect
    them, at the cost of more false alarms (untested).

F14. Consequence for the live distance GUI
    The GUI shows the NEAREST detected object. That is correct for metal,
    plastic, cardboard and plastic-on-metal. But when the real target is
    undetected it does not say "no target": it shows the next detection
    behind it. Felt read ~0.61-0.65 m (beyond the desk), cardboard-on-metal
    ~0.78 m (the metal box's ghost), felt-on-metal ~0.61-0.88 m. A user
    could mistake these for real distances.


4. LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Box orientation assumed (first listed dimension = height).
  - Single mount height (0.51 m), boxes centred only; no lateral offsets,
    tilt, or angle-of-incidence variation.
  - Range-bias compensation uncalibrated (F1); tape reference point (board
    face) approximate.
  - Small trial counts (3 per single box, 2 per stack). Because readings
    are quantised to whole range bins, within-trial standard deviations
    are 0 and do not reflect sub-bin accuracy.
  - Material comparisons are confounded by different top-surface areas and
    distances (F5). Box wall thickness, construction and moisture content
    were not recorded.
  - Cardboard-on-metal trial 2 was not re-placed.
  - Detection rate depends on the chosen window (+/-10 cm around geometric
    distance) and on the fixed CFAR settings.
  - DC range-signature calibration covers out to ~0.35 m. The felt-on-metal
    top (~0.35 m radar reading) sits at the edge of that region. Calibration
    only subtracts the empty-scene signal, but behaviour very close to the
    radar may be affected.
  - Indoor desk environment; objects under or around the desk were not
    controlled (see F10).
